Lesson 1: Introduction to Cybersecurity

Learning Objectives / Manufofin Koyo

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to define cybersecurity, explain why it matters, identify what it protects, recognize basic security risks, and describe the responsibilities of a cybersecurity professional.

A ƙarshen wannan darasi, za ka iya bayyana ma'anar cybersecurity, muhimmancinsa, abubuwan da yake karewa, gane wasu barazanar tsaro, da bayyana ayyukan ƙwararren cybersecurity.

PART A — ENGLISH

1. What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, mobile devices, networks, software, online services and digital information from unauthorized access, misuse, damage, disruption or theft.

It combines people, processes and technology. Security is not achieved by installing one application alone; users must also make careful decisions and organizations must follow safe procedures.

Example: Using a strong, unique password, enabling multi-factor authentication, updating your phone, and refusing suspicious links are basic cybersecurity practices.

2. Why is Cybersecurity Important?

  • Privacy: protects personal and confidential information.
  • Financial safety: helps prevent account theft, payment fraud and financial loss.
  • Availability: helps keep important services working.
  • Trust: helps people and organizations use digital services with confidence.
  • Safety: protects systems that support hospitals, schools, businesses and public services.

3. What Does Cybersecurity Protect?

  • Devices: desktop computers, laptops, tablets and smartphones.
  • Networks: Wi-Fi, office networks and internet-connected systems.
  • Applications: mobile apps, websites and business software.
  • Digital information: passwords, photographs, messages, financial records and medical records.
  • Online accounts: email, social media, banking and cloud accounts.

4. Common Basic Security Risks

  • Phishing: a deceptive message or website designed to trick someone into revealing information or opening a harmful link.
  • Malware: harmful software designed to damage, spy on or gain unauthorized access to a device or data.
  • Password attacks: attempts to guess, steal or reuse passwords to enter accounts.
  • Social engineering: manipulating people into revealing information or taking unsafe actions.
  • Data loss: information becoming unavailable, damaged, deleted or stolen.

5. What Does a Cybersecurity Professional Do?

Cybersecurity professionals help identify risks, protect systems, monitor suspicious activity, investigate incidents, improve security controls and teach users safe practices. They must work legally and obtain permission before testing systems.

6. Practical Example

You receive a message claiming that your bank account will be blocked unless you click a link and enter your password. The message creates urgency and asks for secret information. This may be phishing. Do not click the link or enter your password. Contact the bank through its official app, website or verified phone number.

Lesson 2: History of Cybersecurity

Learning Objectives / Manufofin Koyo

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to describe how cybersecurity developed, explain why early computers needed protection, identify major stages in the history of cyber threats and defenses, and understand how the internet changed security.

A ƙarshen wannan darasi, za ka iya bayyana yadda cybersecurity ya samo asali, dalilin da ya sa kwamfutocin farko suka buƙaci kariya, manyan matakan ci gaban barazanar yanar gizo da kariya, da yadda intanet ta sauya harkar tsaro.

PART A — ENGLISH

1. Why Study the History of Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity did not appear suddenly. It developed as computers became more powerful, connected to one another, and increasingly important to everyday life. Studying its history helps us understand why security controls exist and why attackers and defenders continually adapt.

At first, many computers were large, expensive machines used by a small number of trained people. As computers became smaller and networks expanded, more people could access systems—and the number of possible weaknesses and attacks increased.

2. Early Computing and the Need for Protection

In the early era of computing, security focused heavily on physical protection and controlling who could use a computer. Computers were often kept in restricted rooms. Organizations used procedures, user accounts and access permissions to prevent unauthorized use.

As computers began sharing information through communication networks, protecting the machine itself was no longer enough. Organizations also needed to protect information while it moved between systems.

3. The 1970s and 1980s: Network Research and Early Malware

Research networks connected computers over distance and showed the value—and risk—of connectivity. In 1971, the experimental program known as the Creeper program spread between some systems on ARPANET. It is commonly discussed as an early self-moving experimental program, not as a modern criminal ransomware attack. The Reaper program was created afterward to find and remove Creeper.

During the 1980s, personal computers became more common. The 1986 Brain virus is widely recognized as one of the first viruses for IBM PC-compatible computers. Malware demonstrated that software and removable media could carry threats from one computer to another.

4. The 1990s: The Internet Expands

The public internet and the World Wide Web expanded rapidly. Email, websites and online services created new ways to communicate, but also new ways to spread malicious software, deceive users and attack exposed systems.

Organizations increasingly needed firewalls, antivirus tools, secure configuration, backups and trained staff. Security began to involve whole networks and connected services, not only individual computers.

5. The 2000s: Organized Cybercrime and Wider Attacks

As online banking, shopping and business services grew, criminals increasingly used the internet to steal money, identities and confidential information. Worms, botnets, phishing and large-scale data theft became major concerns.

Security teams developed stronger monitoring, incident response, patch management, encryption and security awareness programs. The goal was not only to prevent attacks, but also to detect them and recover safely.

6. The 2010s to Today: Cloud, Mobile and Advanced Threats

Smartphones, cloud computing, social media, connected devices and remote work have made digital services part of daily life. This has expanded the attack surface: there are more devices, accounts, applications and connections that need protection.

Modern threats include ransomware, credential theft, supply-chain attacks, social engineering and attacks against cloud or internet-connected services. Defenders use multi-factor authentication, encryption, secure software development, backups, threat intelligence and continuous monitoring. Artificial intelligence can support defense, but it can also be misused to make scams more convincing.

7. Important Lesson from History

Technology changes, but several basic security principles remain important: limit access to what people need, keep systems updated, protect sensitive information, make reliable backups, monitor unusual activity, prepare an incident response plan and educate users.

Timeline at a glance
  • Early computing: physical security and controlled access.
  • 1970s: network research and early self-moving programs such as Creeper.
  • 1980s: personal computers and early PC viruses such as Brain.
  • 1990s: wider internet use, email and web-related risks.
  • 2000s: growth of online crime, worms, botnets and phishing.
  • 2010s–today: cloud, mobile, ransomware, connected devices and AI-assisted threats.

PART B — HAUSA

1. Me Ya Sa Za Mu Koyi Tarihin Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity bai bayyana kwatsam ba. Ya bunƙasa ne yayin da kwamfutoci suka ƙara ƙarfi, suka fara haɗuwa da juna, kuma suka zama muhimmi a rayuwar yau da kullum. Koyon tarihinsa yana taimaka mana mu fahimci dalilin da ya sa ake amfani da matakan tsaro, da kuma yadda masu kai hari da masu kare tsarin ke sauya dabarunsu.

A farkon zamanin kwamfuta, manyan kwamfutoci masu tsada ne da ƙwararru kaɗan ke amfani da su. Da kwamfutoci suka zama ƙanana kuma hanyoyin sadarwa suka faɗaɗa, mutane da yawa suka samu damar amfani da su. Hakan ya ƙara yawan wuraren da za a iya samun rauni da kuma yiwuwar kai hari.

2. Kwamfutocin Farko da Bukatar Kariya

A farkon zamanin kwamfuta, tsaro ya fi mayar da hankali kan kare na'urar a zahiri da kuma tantance wanda yake da izinin amfani da ita. Ana ajiye kwamfutoci a ɗakunan da ba kowa ke shiga ba. Ana amfani da dokoki, user accounts da access permissions domin hana shiga ba tare da izini ba.

Da kwamfutoci suka fara musayar bayanai ta networks, kare na'urar kaɗai bai wadatar ba. Sai an kuma kare bayanan yayin da suke tafiya daga wannan na'ura zuwa wata.

3. Shekarun 1970 zuwa 1980: Binciken Networks da Malware na Farko

Hanyoyin sadarwa na bincike sun haɗa kwamfutoci daga wurare daban-daban. Sun nuna amfanin haɗa na'urori, amma kuma haɗin ya kawo sabbin haɗurra. A shekarar 1971, wani shirin gwaji mai suna Creeper ya bazu tsakanin wasu kwamfutoci a ARPANET. Ana yawan ambatonsa a matsayin ɗaya daga cikin shirye-shiryen farko masu iya motsawa tsakanin kwamfutoci; ba ransomware na laifukan zamani ba ne. Daga baya aka ƙirƙiri Reaper domin nemo Creeper da cire shi.

A shekarun 1980, kwamfutocin personal computer suka ƙaru. Brain virus na shekarar 1986 ana ɗaukarsa a matsayin ɗaya daga cikin ƙwayoyin cutar kwamfuta na farko ga IBM PC-compatible computers. Malware ya nuna cewa software ko removable media na iya ɗaukar barazana daga kwamfuta zuwa wata.

4. Shekarun 1990: Faɗaɗar Intanet

Amfani da intanet ga jama'a da World Wide Web ya ƙaru sosai. Email, websites da online services sun sauƙaƙa sadarwa, amma sun kuma buɗe sababbin hanyoyin yaɗa manhajoji masu cutarwa, yaudarar mutane da kai hari ga systems da aka bari a buɗe.

Ƙungiyoyi suka ƙara buƙatar firewalls, antivirus, ingantaccen saitin tsaro, backups da horar da ma'aikata. Tsaro ya fara shafar networks da services gaba ɗaya, ba kwamfuta guda ɗaya kawai ba.

5. Shekarun 2000: Laifukan Intanet da Hare-hare Masu Faɗi

Yayin da online banking, sayayya ta intanet da ayyukan kasuwanci suka bunƙasa, masu laifi suka ƙara amfani da intanet wajen satar kuɗi, identity da bayanan sirri. Worms, botnets, phishing da satar bayanai masu yawa suka zama manyan matsaloli.

Masu tsaro suka bunƙasa sa ido kan systems, amsa hare-hare, sabunta software, encryption da wayar da kan masu amfani. Manufar ba wai hana hari kawai ba ce; har da gano shi da murmurewa cikin aminci.

6. Daga Shekarun 2010 Har Yau: Cloud, Wayoyin Hannu da Barazanar Zamani

Smartphones, cloud computing, social media, na'urorin da ke haɗuwa da intanet da remote work sun sa ayyukan dijital suka zama ɓangare na rayuwar yau da kullum. Wannan ya ƙara yawan wuraren da za a iya kai hari: na'urori, accounts, applications da connections sun ƙaru.

Barazanar yau sun haɗa da ransomware, satar bayanan shiga account, supply-chain attacks, social engineering da hare-hare kan cloud services. Masu kare systems suna amfani da multi-factor authentication, encryption, ingantaccen gina software, backups, bayanan barazana da sa ido akai-akai. Artificial Intelligence na iya taimakawa wajen kariya, amma ana iya amfani da shi wajen ƙara gamsar da saƙonnin yaudara.

7. Muhimmin Darasin da Tarihi Ya Koya Mana

Fasaha tana sauyawa, amma wasu ƙa'idojin tsaro suna nan: a ba mutum izinin abin da yake buƙata kawai, a riƙa sabunta systems, a kare bayanan sirri, a yi backups masu inganci, a sa ido kan abubuwan da ba a saba gani ba, a tanadi tsarin amsa hari, sannan a koyar da masu amfani.

Important Terms — Muhimman Kalmomi

  • ARPANET: ɗaya daga cikin tsoffin hanyoyin sadarwar kwamfuta da suka taimaka wajen bunƙasar intanet.
  • Malware: manhaja mai cutarwa.
  • Computer virus: shirin kwamfuta mai iya yaɗuwa ta hanyar haɗa kansa da wasu fayiloli ko shirye-shirye.
  • Worm: malware da zai iya yaɗuwa tsakanin systems ba tare da haɗa kansa da wani fayil ba.
  • Firewall: tsarin da ke sarrafa zirga-zirgar network bisa ƙa'idojin tsaro.
  • Botnet: tarin na'urorin da aka kamu da malware kuma ake sarrafa su daga nesa ba tare da izinin masu su ba.
  • Ransomware: malware da ke kulle ko ɓoye bayanai sannan ya nemi kuɗi.
  • Incident response: tsarin gano, dakile, bincike da murmurewa bayan aukuwar matsalar tsaro.
  • Patch: sabuntawa da ke gyara kurakurai ko raunin software.

Lesson 3: Types of Cyber Threats

Learning Objectives — Manufofin Koyo

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to identify common cyber threats, explain how they work at a high level, recognize warning signs, and describe basic protective actions.

A ƙarshen darasin, za ka iya gane manyan nau'ikan barazanar cyber, bayyana yadda suke aiki a taƙaice, gano alamomin haɗari, da sanin matakan kariya.

1. What Is a Cyber Threat? — Menene Cyber Threat?

A cyber threat is a possible event, action or condition that could harm a computer, phone, network, application or digital information. A threat may try to steal data, disrupt services, damage files or gain unauthorized access.

Cyber threat barazana ce ko wani abu da zai iya cutar da kwamfuta, waya, network, manhaja ko bayanan dijital. Yana iya ƙoƙarin satar bayanai, dakatar da aiki, lalata files ko shiga ba tare da izini ba.

2. Malware — Malicious Software

Malware means software intentionally designed to harm a device, steal information, spy on users or gain unauthorized access. Malware is a broad category; viruses, worms, Trojans, ransomware and spyware are different types.

Malware na nufin manhajar da aka ƙera da niyyar cutar da na'ura, satar bayanai, leƙen asiri ko shiga ba tare da izini ba. Malware babban rukuni ne da ya ƙunshi virus, worm, Trojan, ransomware da spyware.

Computer Virus: Malware that attaches itself to a legitimate file or program and may spread when that infected host is run.
Hausa: Virus yana manne wa wani file ko program; zai iya yaɗuwa idan aka kunna wannan file ko program ɗin da ya kamu.
Worm: Malware that can copy and spread itself between systems, often across networks, without needing to attach to another file.
Hausa: Worm na iya kwafa kansa ya bazu daga na'ura zuwa wata, musamman ta network, ba tare da buƙatar manne wa wani file ba.
Trojan Horse: Malware disguised as a useful or harmless program to trick someone into installing or running it.
Hausa: Trojan malware ne da ke ɓoye kansa kamar manhaja mai amfani domin yaudarar mutum ya girka ko kunna shi.
Ransomware: Malware that locks a device or encrypts files and demands payment. Paying does not guarantee that files will be restored.
Hausa: Ransomware na kulle na'ura ko ɓoye files sannan ya nemi kuɗi. Biyan kuɗi ba ya tabbatar da dawo da files.
Spyware: Software that secretly monitors activity or collects information.
Hausa: Spyware manhaja ce da ke sa ido a ɓoye ko tattara bayanan mutum ba tare da saninsa ba.
Adware: Software that displays unwanted advertisements; some forms may also track users or create security risks.
Hausa: Adware na nuna tallace-tallacen da ba a so; wasu nau'o'insa na iya bibiyar mai amfani ko jawo haɗarin tsaro.

3. Phishing and Social Engineering

Phishing is a deceptive message, email, call or website designed to trick a person into revealing passwords, bank details or other sensitive information, or into opening a harmful link or attachment.

Phishing saƙo, email, kira ko website na yaudara ne da ake amfani da shi domin mutum ya bayar da password, bayanan banki ko wasu sirrika, ko ya buɗe link ko attachment mai haɗari.

Social engineering is the broader manipulation of people to make them reveal information or take an unsafe action. Phishing is one common form of social engineering.

Social engineering ita ce yaudarar mutum ta hanyar amfani da amincewarsa, tsoro ko gaggawa domin ya bayar da bayani ko ya aikata abin da bai kamata ba. Phishing na ɗaya daga cikin hanyoyinta.

4. Other Important Threats — Wasu Muhimman Barazana

  • DoS / DDoS: An attempt to make a service unavailable by overwhelming it with traffic. DDoS uses many sources. Ƙoƙarin cika sabis da yawan buƙatu har ya kasa amsa wa masu amfani; DDoS yana amfani da na'urori ko wurare da yawa.
  • Password attacks: Attempts to guess, steal or misuse passwords. Ƙoƙarin zato, satar password ko amfani da password da aka samu ba bisa izini ba.
  • Insider threat: Risk caused by someone with authorized access who misuses it, deliberately or accidentally. Haɗarin da ya taso daga mutum mai izinin shiga tsarin wanda ya yi amfani da izinin ba daidai ba, da gangan ko bisa kuskure.
  • Zero-day vulnerability: A software weakness that is exploited before an effective fix is available to the affected users. Raunin software da ake amfani da shi kafin masu amfani su samu ingantaccen gyara.

5. Warning Signs — Alamomin Gargadi

  • Unexpected attachments or links, especially with pressure to act quickly.
  • Requests for passwords, one-time codes or bank details.
  • Unexpected pop-ups, unknown apps or unusual device behavior.
  • Files suddenly renamed, inaccessible or showing ransom messages.
  • Accounts sending messages you did not write or login alerts you do not recognize.

Ka kula da links ko attachments da ba ka zata ba, saƙonnin da ke matsa maka ka yi sauri, neman password ko OTP, apps da ba ka sani ba, files da suka kulle, da sanarwar shiga account da ba kai ka yi ba.

6. How to Reduce the Risk — Yadda Ake Rage Haɗari

  1. Keep your operating system, browser and apps updated.
  2. Use long, unique passwords and a password manager where possible.
  3. Turn on multi-factor authentication (MFA).
  4. Do not open suspicious links or attachments; verify through a trusted official channel.
  5. Install software only from trusted sources.

Lesson 4: Computer Networking Fundamentals

Learning Objectives – Manufofin Darasi

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to explain what a computer network is, identify common network types and devices, understand IP and MAC addresses, and describe basic internet communication.

PART A – ENGLISH EXPLANATION

1. What is a Computer Network?

A computer network is a group of two or more computers or digital devices connected so they can communicate and share information or resources. Devices may connect using cables (wired) or radio signals such as Wi-Fi (wireless).

Example: A home Wi-Fi network connects phones, laptops and smart TVs to a router so they can communicate with one another and access the internet.

Phone   Laptop   Smart TV
↘   ↓   ↙
Wi-Fi Router
↓
Internet Service Provider
↓
Internet

2. Why are Networks Used?

  • Communication: Devices exchange information.
  • Resource sharing: Users can share printers, files and internet connections.
  • Central management: Organizations can manage users and systems centrally.
  • Remote access: Authorized users can access services from other locations.

Security warning: Sharing must be controlled. A network that is not properly secured can expose private files and devices to unauthorized people.

3. Types of Networks

PAN – Personal Area Network: A very small network around one person, such as a phone connected to wireless earbuds using Bluetooth.
LAN – Local Area Network: A network within a limited area, such as a home, office, school or computer laboratory.
MAN – Metropolitan Area Network: A network that can cover a town or city, often connecting multiple LANs.
WAN – Wide Area Network: A network covering large geographical areas. The internet is the best-known example of a global network of networks.

Remember: These names describe the general geographical scope of a network. Real networks can be designed in different ways.

4. Common Network Devices

  • Router: Connects different networks and forwards data between them. A home router commonly connects the home network to the internet.
  • Switch: Connects devices inside a wired LAN and forwards local network traffic to the appropriate device.
  • Modem: Connects to an internet service provider's access technology and converts signals as required by that connection. Some home devices combine a modem and router.
  • Wireless Access Point (AP): Allows wireless devices to join a network, usually through Wi-Fi.
  • Network Interface Card (NIC): Hardware that enables a device to connect to a network, through Ethernet or Wi-Fi.

5. Wired and Wireless Connections

Wired networking: Uses physical cables, commonly Ethernet. It can provide stable and fast connections, but devices need a cable connection.

Wireless networking: Uses radio signals, such as Wi-Fi or Bluetooth. It offers mobility, but signal quality and security settings matter.

6. IP Address and MAC Address

IP address (Internet Protocol address): A logical address used to identify an interface on an IP network so data can be delivered to the right network destination. IPv4 addresses look like 192.168.1.10. IPv6 addresses are longer and use hexadecimal characters.

MAC address (Media Access Control address): A link-layer address associated with a network interface. It is commonly shown as six pairs of hexadecimal digits, for example 00:1A:2B:3C:4D:5E. Some devices use randomized MAC addresses for privacy.

Key difference: An IP address is used for communication at the network layer and may change depending on the network. A MAC address is used on the local network link and identifies a network interface there. Neither should be treated as a password or proof of a person's identity.

7. DNS and DHCP

DNS – Domain Name System: Helps translate human-readable domain names, such as example.com, into IP addresses used by computers to locate services.

DHCP – Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol: Automatically provides network settings to devices, commonly including an IP address, subnet information, default gateway and DNS server.

Simple example: When a phone joins Wi-Fi, DHCP may give it a local IP address. When the user enters a website name, DNS helps the phone find the address of that website's server.

8. TCP, UDP and Ports

TCP – Transmission Control Protocol: A transport protocol that establishes a connection and provides reliable, ordered delivery of data.

UDP – User Datagram Protocol: A transport protocol that sends datagrams without setting up a TCP-style connection. It has lower overhead, but does not itself guarantee delivery or order.

Port number: A number used by transport protocols to direct traffic to the correct application or service on a device. A port is not a physical socket.

  • HTTPS: Commonly uses TCP port 443; modern HTTP/3 uses QUIC over UDP port 443.
  • DNS: Commonly uses port 53 over UDP or TCP, depending on the operation.
  • SSH: Commonly uses TCP port 22 for secure remote administration.

9. What Happens When You Open a Website?

  1. Your device connects to a network, often using Wi-Fi or mobile data.
  2. It uses DNS to find the IP address associated with the website name, if it is not already known.
  3. Your device sends network traffic toward the server, through routers and other network equipment.
  4. The browser and server communicate using the relevant web protocols. HTTPS protects the connection using encryption and authentication.
  5. The server sends data back and your browser displays the page.

10. Basic Network Security

  • Use WPA2-AES or WPA3 security on Wi-Fi where supported; avoid open Wi-Fi for sensitive activity.
  • Change default router administrator passwords.
  • Keep router firmware and connected devices updated.
  • Use a strong, unique Wi-Fi password and do not share it publicly.
  • Turn off features such as remote administration if they are not needed.
  • Only scan or test networks that you own or have clear permission to assess.

Learning Objectives – Manufofin Darasi

PART A – ENGLISH EXPLANATION

1. What is a Command-Line Interface (CLI)?

A Command-Line Interface (CLI) is a text-based way of communicating with an operating system. Instead of selecting icons and menus, a user types a command and the system responds with text.

A terminal is the application or window where you type commands. A shell is the program that reads commands and asks the operating system to carry them out. Windows Command Prompt (CMD), PowerShell, and common Linux shells such as Bash are examples of command environments.

User types a command
↓
Terminal displays the session
↓
Shell interprets the command
↓
Operating system performs an allowed task
↓
Result appears on screen

Hausa: CLI hanya ce ta amfani da rubutattun umarni domin sadarwa da Operating System. Terminal shi ne taga ko manhajar da ake rubuta umarni a ciki. Shell kuma shi ne shirin da ke karanta umarnin ya miƙa shi ga Operating System.

2. Opening a Command Window

Windows: Open the Start menu, type cmd to open Command Prompt, or type PowerShell to open PowerShell. You do not need administrator mode for the beginner commands in this lesson.

Linux: Open the Terminal application from the applications menu. On many desktop distributions, Ctrl + Alt + T opens a terminal, but the shortcut may differ.

Hausa: A Windows, ka buɗe Start menu ka rubuta cmd ko PowerShell. A Linux, ka buɗe manhajar Terminal. Kada ka buɗe da Administrator ko sudo sai idan akwai takamaiman dalili kuma ka fahimci abin da umarnin zai yi.

3. Understanding Command Structure

A command often contains a command name, an option (also called a flag or switch), and sometimes a target such as a file or folder.

Example: ping -n 4 127.0.0.1
ping is the command; -n 4 asks Windows to send four requests; 127.0.0.1 is the computer's own loopback address.

Commands can behave differently in CMD, PowerShell and Linux. Do not assume that a command or option works in every shell. Spaces, quotation marks and spelling matter. A path containing spaces should usually be placed inside quotation marks.

Hausa: Umurni na iya ƙunsar sunan command, ƙarin zaɓi (flag), da sunan file ko folder. Umarnin CMD, PowerShell da Linux ba lallai su zama iri ɗaya ba. Haruffa, tazara da alamun quotation suna da muhimmanci.

6. Files, Folders and Paths

A directory is another name for a folder. A path describes where a file or directory is located.

  • Windows absolute path: C:\Users\Student\Documents
  • Linux absolute path: /home/student/Documents
  • Relative path: A location described from the directory you are currently in, such as Documents/notes.txt.
  • Current directory: The folder your command session is currently using.
  • Parent directory: The folder one level above the current directory.

Some files are hidden by default. In Linux, hidden filenames commonly begin with a dot, such as .config. Windows also has hidden-file attributes. Hidden does not mean secure; permissions and proper protection are still required.

Hausa: Directory na nufin folder. Path kuma adireshin da ke nuna inda file ko folder yake. Absolute path yana nuna cikakken adireshi daga tushen drive ko tsarin fayil; relative path kuma yana dogara da folder ɗin da kake ciki. Hidden file ba yana nufin file ɗin yana da kariya ta musamman ba.

7. Users and File Permissions

Permissions control what a user or program may do with a file or directory. In Linux, the common permission letters are:

  • r – Read: Permission to view or read content.
  • w – Write: Permission to change content.
  • x – Execute: Permission to run a program or, for a directory, access/search it.

Linux permissions can apply to the file owner, a group, and other users. The command ls -l displays permission information. For example, rwxr-xr-- describes read/write/execute for the owner, read/execute for the group, and read-only for others.

chmod changes permissions. For example, chmod u+x myscript.sh adds execute permission for the file owner. Only practise this on a file you created in your own practice folder. Do not change permissions on system files.

Windows also uses file and folder permissions, commonly managed through Properties → Security. An Administrator account can make system-wide changes, so use a Standard User account for ordinary tasks when possible.

Hausa: Permissions suna ƙayyade abin da mutum ko program zai iya yi da file. Read karantawa ne; Write gyarawa ko rubutawa ne; Execute gudanar da program ne. A Linux, izini na iya shafar mai file, group da sauran masu amfani. Kada ka canza izinin system files ba tare da ƙwarewa da izini ba.

8. Using the Command Line for Cybersecurity

Security learners use command-line tools to inspect their own systems. Examples include:

  • whoami – Confirm which account is active.
  • hostname – Identify the device name.
  • ipconfig on Windows or ip addr on Linux – Inspect local network configuration.
  • tasklist on Windows or ps on Linux – Review running processes.
  • ping 127.0.0.1 – Test the local TCP/IP loopback interface. This does not test your internet connection.

These commands help you observe and understand a system. They do not prove that a computer is completely secure. A normal-looking process list or network address is not a full security audit.

Hausa: Masu koyon cybersecurity suna amfani da commands domin duba kwamfutarsu, account, network da programs da ke gudana. Amma sakamakon commands kaɗai ba ya tabbatar da cewa kwamfuta tana da cikakken tsaro.

9. Command-Line Safety Rules

  1. Use commands only on devices and networks you own or have clear permission to test.
  2. Read and understand a command before pressing Enter.
  3. Do not paste commands from unknown websites, messages or videos.
  4. Do not run a command as Administrator or with sudo unless it is necessary and you understand its effect.
  5. Be especially careful with commands that delete, overwrite, format, change permissions recursively, or modify disks.
  6. Back up important files before making system changes.
  7. If a command asks for a password or elevated access unexpectedly, stop and investigate.
Golden rule – Muhimmin ƙa'ida: Never run a command just because someone says it is safe. Understand what it does, what it changes, and whether you have permission.

10. Practical Activities – Ayyukan Gwaji

These activities are designed to inspect information and create one practice folder. They do not require administrator privileges.

  1. Windows: Open CMD and run whoami. Write down what the output tells you.
  2. Windows: Run hostname and ipconfig. Identify the computer name and the network adapter information. Do not share private network details publicly.
  3. Windows: Run mkdir Cybersecurity-Practice, then dir to confirm that the folder appears. If it already exists, do not create another one.
  4. Linux: Run pwd, then ls, and identify your current directory and its visible contents.
  5. Linux: Run whoami and hostname to identify the current account and device.
  6. Explain in your own words why you should not run unknown commands with Administrator or sudo privileges.

Hausa: Ka fara da duba bayanai kawai. A Windows, gwada whoami, hostname, ipconfig, da ƙirƙirar folder na gwaji. A Linux, gwada pwd, ls, whoami da hostname. Kada ka yi amfani da kwamfutar wani ko network ɗin wani ba tare da izini ba.

11. Important Terms – Muhimman Kalmomi

  • CLI: Command-Line Interface – hanyar amfani da rubutattun umarni.
  • Terminal: Taga ko manhajar rubuta commands.
  • Shell: Shirin da ke karanta commands ya aiwatar da su ta Operating System.
  • Command: Umarnin da ake ba kwamfuta.
  • Directory: Folder ko babban fayil.
  • Path: Adireshin da ke nuna wurin file ko folder.
  • Process: Program ko aiki da ke gudana.
  • Permission: Izinin karantawa, gyarawa ko gudanar da abu.
  • Privilege: Matsayin iko ko izini da account yake da shi.
  • Loopback address: Adireshin da na'ura ke amfani da shi wajen sadarwa da kanta, kamar 127.0.0.1.

12. Lesson Summary – Takaitaccen Darasi

The command line provides a text-based way to inspect and manage an operating system. Windows CMD and Linux terminals have commands for navigating folders, viewing system details, checking network configuration and observing processes. Paths identify file locations, while permissions control access. A cybersecurity learner must use these tools carefully, with authorization, and avoid unknown or destructive commands.

Command line hanya ce ta amfani da rubutattun umarni wajen duba da sarrafa Operating System. CMD na Windows da Terminal na Linux suna da commands na zagayawa cikin folders, duba bayanan kwamfuta, network da processes. Path yana nuna wurin file; permissions kuma suna sarrafa izini. Dalibin cybersecurity dole ya yi amfani da waɗannan tools cikin hankali, da izini, kuma ya guji commands da ba ya fahimta ko masu iya lalata bayanai.
